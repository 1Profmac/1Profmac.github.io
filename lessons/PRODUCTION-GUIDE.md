# Production Guide

How to deliver these scripts so they sound like 50+TechBridge and not like a bank compliance video.

---

## Voice

You are talking to a capable adult who has been talked down to.

**Do**

- Use short sentences. Then stop.
- Say “you” more than “seniors,” “the elderly,” or “folks.”
- Admit what is hard. Then show the next step.
- Use one number, then a human meaning. Never stack four statistics in a row.
- Smile with the eyes, not with a punchline at their expense.

**Do not**

- Say “it’s easy!” It is not easy the first time. It is *doable*.
- Say “even Grandma can do this.”
- Use the word “simple” as a stick. “Plain English” is better.
- Rush the silence after a hard sentence. That silence is the lesson landing.
- Pretend AI is magic or pretend it is the enemy. It is a tool.

Brian’s register is the house style: warm, direct, a little stubborn about dignity. Guest instructors match the values, not the biography. If you are not 65 and you do not run a company with AI, do not say those lines. Say: “Our founder, Brian McKinney, is 65 and runs his company with these tools. He built this lesson so you would not have to figure it out alone.”

## Two runtimes, one lesson

| Version | Clock | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short | 3:00–4:30 | Social, email, QR landing, live-session cold open |
| Long | 12:00–16:00 | Course video, YouTube, in-person core |

Long scripts include `[PAUSE]`, `[ON SCREEN]`, `[SHOW]`, and `[B-ROLL]` cues. If you cut a cue, keep the spoken sentence that carries the idea.

Spoken pace: **130–140 words per minute**. The long scripts are written to that pace with pauses. If you run hot, you are teaching fear.

---

## On-screen type

Adults 50+ should not squint.

- Minimum **42–48 px** equivalent on a 1080p frame for key lines. Larger is better.
- High contrast. Dark navy `#083655` or black on white, or white on `#083655`. Accent orange `#e2752e` for one word, not a paragraph.
- One idea per card. Six words is better than sixteen.
- Lower-third name/title stays up for **five seconds**, not two.
- Burned-in captions **and** a caption file. Many watch on a phone in a bright kitchen.

Every lesson has a **fridge line** — one sentence they can photograph.

| Lesson | Fridge line |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | You are not too late. You just needed the right teacher. |
| 2 | One tool. One real question. One useful answer. |
| 3 | Unexpected + hurry + money or a code = hang up and verify. |

## Accessibility is the standard

- Open captions on every video.
- Transcript posted with the lesson (the long script *is* the transcript — clean the cues if you publish it).
- Describe what is on screen when the demo matters: “I am typing in the white box at the bottom.”
- Do not rely on color alone to mark a bad link.
- Offer a still of the final checklist at the end of Lessons 2 and 3.
- Live room: repeat questions from the floor. Face the learners, not only the screen.
- Spanish: we already print Spanish flyers and postcards. Commission a **adaptation**, not a word-for-word translation. Humor, examples, and scam types shift.

## Filming Lesson 1 — Welcome

Talking head is enough if the eyes are kind.

B-roll that helps: a library table, hands on a phone, two adults looking at one screen, Austin Public Library exterior if you have permission. No stock “confused senior pointing at a laptop.” We do not sell that story.

End on the map of three lessons. Hold it.

---

## Filming Lesson 2 — Talk to AI

This lesson **fails** if they only hear you talk about AI.

- Screen-record the real tool at **150% zoom** or larger.
- Use **ChatGPT Free** as the default (matches our flyers). Say Microsoft Copilot is fine if it is already on their Windows PC. Do not tour six brands.
- The question must be real and boring. Best classroom question:

  > “I am 68. I have leftover roast chicken, rice, and spinach. Give me three easy dinners for two people. No spicy food. List ingredients I still need and the steps in short sentences.”

- Show a weak prompt (“dinners”) and a strong prompt (the paragraph above). Same tool. Different result. That is the whole skill.
- Then ask a follow-up: “Make the second dinner even simpler. I only want one pan.”
- Point to the rule card **before** anyone types: no Social Security numbers, no bank logins, no passwords.
- If the tool hallucinates, **keep the take**. Say “this part I would check.” That is the advanced skill.

Live session: one shared screen. Learners who are ready type on their own device. Pair people. Never leave someone staring at a login wall alone — have a staff login for the demo account.

---

## Filming Lesson 3 — Don't get scammed

Use **labeled fake** examples.

- Fake From: `amazon-security@email-secure-alert.com`
- Fake text: “Your Social Security will be suspended in 30 minutes. Call 1-888-555-0147.”
- Fake grandchild call: audio of *you* doing a bad urgent voice, then: “That can be a recording now. That is why we use a code word.”

Never display a real password, a real QR code that goes somewhere unknown, or a real 2FA code.

Do not perform panic. Perform **the pause**. The hero of this lesson is the person who hangs up.

Close on agency: the FTC’s own data says most older adults who report a scam **lost no money**. They spotted it. Then give the two URLs.

## Live-session extras (not in the short video)

If you have 60–90 minutes:

1. Play or read the short script as the cold open.
2. Teach from the long script.
3. Do the action in the room (Lesson 2: one prompt; Lesson 3: turn on 2FA on a throwaway or walk through screenshots).
4. Hand out [learner-take-home.md](learner-take-home.md).
5. Sign-in sheet at the **start**. Photos only with permission. See the instructor package.

Ground rules on the wall:

- No one gets left behind.
- There are no stupid questions.
- We do not shame anyone who has been scammed. We help them lock the door.

---

## What we never do

- Invent urgency to enroll (“spots are vanishing”).
- Sell a password manager or crypto product from the lesson.
- Claim we can recover stolen money.
- Use a learner’s real scam as entertainment without written consent.
- Stack fear on fear and call it education.

We are funded to make people **more free**. Fear without a next step is another scam.
