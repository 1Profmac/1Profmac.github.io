# Lesson 1 — Welcome

**Version:** Long form  
**Runtime:** 14–16 minutes  
**Speaker:** Brian McKinney (or a trained 50+TechBridge instructor using the alternate intro)  
**On-screen title:** Welcome. You are not too late.  
**Fridge line:** You are not too late. You just needed the right teacher.

**Objective:** The learner feels they belong, understands the three-lesson map, and knows the one thing to do before Lesson 2.

**Tone:** Warm. Unhurried. No pep-rally. Dignity first.

## Who this is for

This free series is for you if any of these are true.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
This is for you if…

You are frustrated with a phone that keeps changing.

You want to stay useful at work — or find work — and the job ads all say “AI.”

You want to talk to a doctor on a screen without feeling foolish.

You want to see a grandchild’s face and not miss the button.

You are tired of scam texts and you do not want to be the person who “should have known.”

Or you are here because someone you love sent you the link and said, “Just try it.”

All of those are good reasons.

You do not need experience. You do not need a new computer. You do not need to become a different person.

You need a teacher who will not rush you, and one win you can feel.

## You are not starting from zero

I want to put a fact in your pocket, because the culture is loud and the culture is wrong.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
90% of adults 50+ already own a smartphone.  
— AARP, 2025–2026

Nine out of ten adults over 50 already own a smartphone. That is AARP’s research, not a guess. Ten years ago it was barely half.

You text. A lot of you stream a show. A lot of you already talk to a little voice in the car that reads a text out loud. That voice is AI. You have been using pieces of this for years. Nobody bothered to tell you the name.

`[PAUSE]`

Here is the other number.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Generative AI use, adults 50+  
2023: 9%  
2024: 18%  
2025: 30%  
— AARP

In 2023, about nine percent of people our age had tried the new kind of AI — the kind you type a question into.

A year later, eighteen percent.

The year after that, thirty percent.

That is not teenagers. That is us. The door is already open. Most of the people who walked through it still call themselves beginners.

Beginner is an honest word. It is not an insult.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Beginner is a fine place to be.

AARP also found that about eight in ten adults over 50 have used *some* form of AI — the helpful kind that finishes a sentence or answers when you say “Hey.” Only a tiny sliver call themselves experts. So if you feel behind, you are standing in a very large crowd.

The people who look fluent on television have had time, and a teacher, and usually a job that paid them to practice.

You are allowed to have those things too.

Digital independence is not a privilege.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Digital independence is not a privilege.  
It is your right.

It is your right to read a health portal, pay a bill, write a letter, check a rumor, and decide for yourself.

Ageism is real. It shows up in hiring. It shows up in product design. It shows up in the joke that you cannot teach someone our age a new trick.

We are done accepting that joke.

## What these three lessons are

This is not a college course. It is not a tour of twenty apps. It is three short lessons with one job each.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
The map

**Lesson 1 — Welcome**  
You belong here. Here is the map.

**Lesson 2 — Talk to AI**  
Ask one real question in one tool. Get a useful answer.

**Lesson 3 — Don’t get scammed**  
Spot a fake. Name a pressure tactic. Lock the door.

That is the whole free series.

Lesson 2 is the one people are nervous about. I will tell you the secret now, so your shoulders can drop.

You will pick **one** tool. I recommend the free version of ChatGPT because it is the one we can show on a website without buying anything. If your Windows computer already has Copilot, that is fine too. We will not hop between brands.

You will ask **one** question from your real life. Not a trick question. Something you actually need — a dinner, a letter, a list, an explanation of a paragraph you do not understand.

You will leave with **one** useful answer.

If the first answer is messy, you will ask a follow-up: “Make that simpler.” That is the whole skill. We will practice it on screen.

`[PAUSE]`

Lesson 3 exists because the other fear in the room is fraud.

I will not scare you for sport. I will give you three habits the Federal Trade Commission and the FBI keep repeating because they work.

You will learn how to spot a fake message.

You will learn the pressure tactics — hurry, fear, secrecy, “move your money to protect it.”

You will learn what a good password looks like now, and what two-factor authentication is, in English.

And we will talk about fake voices. That is new. Families need a code word. We will do that without drama.

Then we will tell you exactly where to report a scam if one has already happened. Shame is optional. Reporting is not a confession. It is how they catch the next one.

## How to use this series

A few house rules. They will make you faster, not slower.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
How to use these lessons

**Pause me.**  
This is not live TV. If a sentence is thick, stop. Replay it.

**One device is enough.**  
Phone, tablet, or computer. Whatever you already have.

**Write one thing down.**  
A question you want to ask in Lesson 2. A person you will tell about Lesson 3.

**You can watch with someone.**  
A spouse. A friend. A grown child — if they can keep their hands off your phone.

**You can watch alone.**  
Some people think better without an audience. That is allowed.

If a word is new, I will say it twice and put it on the screen. You do not have to memorize jargon to be good at this.

Artificial intelligence, in this series, means a tool that can read your words and write a useful reply. That is enough definition for today.

## What we will never do

I want you to hear this before you give us an hour of your life.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
What we never do

We will not rush you.

We will not sell you a gadget from this lesson.

We will not ask for your Social Security number, your bank login, or a code from your phone.

We will not use scare tactics to make you enroll in something.

The real Federal Trade Commission will not do that either. We will come back to that in Lesson 3.

## The feeling we are after

People think the product is software.

It is not.

When I listen to adults our age — in forums, in rooms, in the quiet after class — I hear the same five sentences.

“Is it too late for me?”

“Nobody explains it at my level.”

“I feel stupid when I try.”

“My family doesn’t have time.”

“I’m afraid I’ll get scammed.”

`[PAUSE]`

Those are not technology problems. Those are dignity problems with a login screen attached.

AARP found that three in five adults over 50 say technology is not designed with their age in mind. Of course you feel like the room was built for someone else. In many ways, it was.

Our job is not to make you love every app.

Our job is to give you enough skill and enough safety that the world cannot shut you out.

Researchers who study AI classes for older adults keep finding the same thing we find in the library: people do not want a lecture on how the engine works. They want to try one real task, with a patient human nearby, and feel the win in their hands.

That is Lesson 2.

Then they want to know they will not lose the house because they clicked something. That is Lesson 3.

## Your homework — today, not someday

Before you open Lesson 2, do one small thing.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Do this today

Write down **one real question** from your life this week.

Not a perfect question. A real one.

Examples, if your mind goes blank:

- “Explain this doctor’s note in plain English.” You can hide the name.
- “I have chicken, rice, and spinach. Give me three easy dinners.”
- “Help me write a polite email to my HOA about the parking.”
- “I am applying for a job after twenty years. Give me ten interview questions and short sample answers.”
- “Plan a Saturday in Austin for two people who hate walking more than a mile.”

Put the question on a sticky note. Photograph it. That sticky note is your ticket to Lesson 2.

If you cannot think of a question, write this: “I feel behind. Give me three ways an adult my age can use AI this week without sharing private information.”

That counts.
