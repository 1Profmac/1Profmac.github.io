## Script

This is Lesson 3. Three skills. Then you go lock one door.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
1 Spot a fake  
2 Name the pressure  
3 Passwords and 2FA

The Federal Trade Commission says adults 60 and older reported $2.4 billion lost in 2024 — about four times 2020. The FBI’s internet center says people over 60 lost nearly five billion.

Here is the part that should stay with you: about three out of four older adults who reported a scam to the FTC **lost no money**. They spotted it. That can be you.

**Spot a fake.** Look at who started the conversation. Look at the real email address, not the logo. A surprise link, a number *they* gave you, a QR code you did not ask for — those are costumes. AI cleaned up the spelling. “No typos” is no longer a test.

**Name the pressure.** Hurry. Fear. Secrecy. A fake badge. And the line that empties retirement accounts: “Move your money to protect it.” The FTC is tired of that one. Never wire, never gift cards, never a Bitcoin ATM, never a courier, because a stranger said your account was in danger.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Hang up. Look up. Call back.

Hang up. Call the bank or the agency on the number on your statement — not the number in the text.

A voice that sounds like family is not proof anymore. Cloned voices are cheap. AARP finds most people our age already worry about this. Pick a **family code word**. If the panicked caller does not know it, you hang up and you dial the number in *your* phone.

**Lock the door.** NIST says make the password **long** — a phrase, fifteen characters or more — and stop reusing the email password on the bank. Write phrases in a notebook at home if you need to.

Then turn on **2FA** — two-factor authentication — on email first. Password plus a code on your phone. CISA says any second lock is better than none.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Never read that code to anyone

If a helper, a banker, or a cop on the phone wants the code that just arrived, they are not a helper. The FTC said so.

`[ON SCREEN]`  
Unexpected + hurry + money or a code  
= hang up and verify

If money already moved: stop, call the number on the card, then report at reportfraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov. Tell one person you trust. Shame helps them. Reporting helps you.

I’m Brian McKinney. You are allowed to hang up.
