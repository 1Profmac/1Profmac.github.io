# The Eleven Labs

**50+TechBridge · Digital Pioneer Core**  
Facilitator scripts for the eleven hands-on wins. Same voice as the free-lesson scripts. Same house rules: one tool, their device, no shame, no theory dump.

These are the **You do** blocks. Pair each lab with the 110-minute session script (`forms/facilitator/00-session-script.html`): Arrive → One story, one rule → **I do** → **We do** → **You do** → Save the win → Exit slip.

Welcome is not a lab. It is ten minutes at the start of Lab 1.

---

## How the eleven map to six weeks

| Lab | Title | Week | Who gets it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ask one real question | 1 Talk to AI | Everyone |
| 2 | Steer the answer | 1 Talk to AI | Everyone |
| 3 | Spot a fake | 2 Don’t get scammed | Everyone |
| 4 | Hang up and the code word | 2 Don’t get scammed | Everyone |
| 5 | Lock the door (password + 2FA) | 2 Don’t get scammed | Everyone |
| 6 | Make one video call | 3 Stay connected | Everyone |
| 7 | Draft a note, then send it | 3 Stay connected | Everyone |
| 8 | Health without handing over the keys | 4 Health & independence | Everyone |
| 9 | Safer money | 5 Fork A | Library / retired |
| 10 | Job or workplace | 5 Fork B or C | WIOA or employer |
| 11 | Show the win | 6 Your next chapter | Everyone |

**Do not teach Labs 9 and 10 in the same room.** Circle one fork on the Week 5 sheet.

A 110-minute week can hold **two labs** if they are short (1+2, 6+7). Week 2 can hold 3+4 in the room and Lab 5 as take-home if you run out of time. Lab 5 is the one that must not stay “someday.”

---

## House rules (read once, keep forever)

- One AI tool for the whole cohort. ChatGPT Free is the default. Copilot if it is already on their Windows PC.
- Never paste: Social Security number, bank or Medicare numbers, passwords, phone codes, someone else’s private business.
- AI can be confidently wrong. Health, money, and the law are drafts. A human who is qualified decides.
- Their phone or laptop. Not a lab PC if you can help it.
- Never “even grandma.” Never grab the phone out of their hand.
- Gift cards, wires, crypto ATMs, couriers, remote access, “move money to protect it” = the lesson is over. Hang up.

**Exit slip (every lab):**  
1. Confidence 1–5 doing this alone.  
2. One thing I will do again this week.  
3. What still feels unclear.

# Lab 1 — Ask one real question

**Week:** 1 · Talk to AI  
**Clock:** 30 minutes You do (after I do / We do)  
**End state:** They asked one real question in one tool and can read a useful answer back.

### One story, one rule
Story: You are not too late. Most adults 50+ already have a smartphone.  
Rule: **The AI can be wrong. You decide if the answer is useful.**

### I do (say this)
“I am opening one box. I type who I am, what I need, and what the answer should look like.”

Type slowly, out loud:

> I am 68. I cook for two. I have leftover roast chicken, rice, and spinach. Give me three easy dinners. No spicy food. List extra ingredients and short steps.

Then: “I understand this. I could cook the first one. That is a useful answer.”

### We do
Everyone types this starter on the board:

> I’m new to AI. I’m over 50. Explain ________ in 5 short steps. Use plain language. Do not give medical or legal advice.

Wait for every screen. Do not open a second app.

### You do
They use **their** sticky-note question from Welcome. If they have none:

> I feel behind. Give me three ways an adult my age can use AI this week without sharing private information.

**Done when:** they can read the answer back in their own words.

### If they are stuck
No email → classroom account or pair.  
Tiny text → enlarge the font, then come back.  
Afraid to type → they dictate the first line; they type the rest.

### Take-home
Screenshot or write the useful answer. Bring it to Lab 2.

# Lab 2 — Steer the answer

**Week:** 1 · Talk to AI  
**Clock:** 20–25 minutes  
**End state:** They asked one follow-up and the answer got more useful.

### One story, one rule
Story: The first answer is a draft. You already do this when you give a neighbor better directions.  
Rule: **You are not being rude. You are steering.**

### I do
On the chicken dinners, type:

> Make the second dinner even simpler. I only want one pan. I do not have cream. Use words a tired person can follow at 6 p.m.

### We do
Pick one follow-up as a class. Everyone sends the same line:

> Make that simpler.

### You do
They send **one** of these on their Lab 1 answer:

- Make that simpler.
- Give me three options.
- I have 15 minutes. Cut this down.
- Explain the second step like I have never done it.

**Useful test (say it):** I understand it. I could do the next step. Nothing private was required.

If it sounds too sure — a phone number, a dose, an “always” — they check that part or throw it out.

### Take-home
Write the follow-up they used. That sentence is the skill.

# Lab 3 — Spot a fake

**Week:** 2 · Don’t get scammed  
**Clock:** 25 minutes  
**End state:** They can mark four tells on a fake message and say who started the conversation.

### One story, one rule
Story: About three in four older adults who reported a scam to the FTC lost no money. They spotted it.  
Rule: **Look at the costume, not the logo. “No typos” is no longer a test.**

Use **labeled FAKE** examples only. Never a real password or QR code that goes somewhere unknown.

### I do
Show a fake email: `amazon-security@email-secure-alert.com` — “Your account will close in 20 minutes.”

Ask out loud:

1. Who started this?
2. Does the real address look odd?
3. Are they sending me to a link or a number *they* gave me?
4. Would my bank already know my name?

### We do
Same four questions on a fake text: “Social Security will be suspended in 30 minutes. Call this number.”

The real FTC will never threaten you, tell you to move money to “protect it,” or demand gift cards.

### You do
Each person gets a printed fake (or one on the projector). They circle the tells and write: **I started this / they started this.**

**Done when:** they can say “they started it” and name one tell.

### Take-home
Fridge line starts here: *Unexpected + hurry + money or a code = hang up and verify.*

# Lab 4 — Hang up and the code word

**Week:** 2 · Don’t get scammed  
**Clock:** 20 minutes  
**End state:** They can say the three steps and they have a family code word written down.

### One story, one rule
Story: A familiar voice is no longer proof. Cloned voices are cheap.  
Rule: **Hang up. Look up. Call back — on a number you already had.**

### I do
Act the bad urgent voice for five seconds. Then: “That can be a recording now. That is why we use a word the scammer does not know.”

Pressure levers on the board: **Hurry. Fear. Secrecy. Authority. Move money to protect it.**

Stop if they want: gift cards, wire, crypto ATM, a courier, remote control of the computer, the code that just arrived on the phone.

### We do
Practice out loud, as a class:

“I am going to hang up. I will call the number on the back of my card.”

### You do
1. Write the three steps on the take-home card.
2. Write a **family code word** they would never put on Facebook.
3. Write the names of the two people who will know it.

**Done when:** the word is on paper, not only in the air.

### Take-home
Cut-out code-word card from `lessons/learner-take-home.md`. Tell those two people this week. Do not post the word online.

# Lab 5 — Lock the door

**Week:** 2 · Don’t get scammed  
**Clock:** 30 minutes  
**End state:** Email has 2FA **or** they have a written password plan they will finish tonight. They know never to read a code to anyone.

### One story, one rule
Story: Email is the skeleton key. If they get email, they can reset the bank.  
Rule: **NIST: longer is better. CISA: any second lock is better than none. FTC: anyone who asks for the code is a scammer.**

### I do
On screen (settings only — no real secret): Settings → Security → Two-factor / 2-step / Multifactor.

Say: “Password plus something I have. I type the code into the site I opened. I never read it to a stranger.”

Show a passphrase shape, not a real one: `maple-porch-radio-Thursday` — 15+ characters.

### We do
Everyone finds the Security page on email. Stop before they share a code out loud.

### You do
Pick one and finish it in the room if you can:

1. Turn on 2FA on **email**. Then the bank at home.
2. Or write three unique phrases in a **notebook** that lives in a drawer or lockbox — not on the monitor.

**Done when:** 2FA is on, or the notebook plan has a when and a where.

### Take-home
Screenshot of 2FA on, or the written plan. Never photograph the actual phrases into an unlocked camera roll.

# Lab 6 — Make one video call

**Week:** 3 · Stay connected  
**Clock:** 30 minutes  
**End state:** They placed or received one video call on **one** app.

### One story, one rule
Story: The product is a face, not an app.  
Rule: **One calling app for the whole room.** Zoom, FaceTime, or Google Meet — pick before you walk in.

### I do
Join the room link slowly. Point: mute, camera, leave. Narrate every tap.

### We do
Everyone joins the same test call. Wait. Wave. Leave. Come back.

### You do
They call a partner in the room **or** a person they already have in their phone. Two minutes. Then hang up on purpose so they know they can.

**Done when:** they have been on a live call and they know how to leave it.

### If they are stuck
No account → pair with a neighbor.  
Camera off is allowed. Voice is still a win.  
Do not teach a second calling app.

### Take-home
Call one person this week. Write who.

# Lab 7 — Draft a note, then send it

**Week:** 3 · Stay connected  
**Clock:** 25 minutes  
**End state:** They drafted a short message with AI, edited it in their own words, and sent it.

### One story, one rule
Story: The AI writes a draft. You sound like you.  
Rule: **If you would not say it at the kitchen table, do not send it.**

Same AI tool as Week 1.

### I do

> Write a short, kind message to my grandchild about missing Sunday dinner. 4 sentences. Plain language. No slang I would not use.

Then edit one sentence out loud so it sounds like you.

### We do
Same prompt, blank in the middle: “about ________.”

### You do
They fill the blank (coworker, friend, HOA, thank-you). They edit. They send — email, text, or Messenger. **They** press send.

**Done when:** a real message left their device.

### Safety
No medical complaints that name a diagnosis. No account numbers. No angry all-caps to an HOA unless they meant it after a night’s sleep.

### Take-home
Keep the prompt. Use it again Friday.

# Lab 8 — Health without handing over the keys

**Week:** 4 · Health & independence  
**Clock:** 35 minutes  
**End state:** They opened a portal **or** wrote three questions for a visit. They can say: AI is not my doctor.

### One story, one rule
Story: Independence is a refill, a portal, a question list — not a diagnosis from a chatbot.  
Rule: **Verify with a clinician. If the AI names a diagnosis, a dose, or “skip the visit,” discard it.**

### I do
Show a portal home screen **or** (if no portal in the room) the prompt only:

> Help me write 3 questions to ask my doctor about ________. Do not diagnose. Do not suggest I skip a visit. Use plain language.

Cover names and record numbers before anyone pastes a portal email.

### We do
Class writes three questions about a **made-up** topic (sore knee, sleep, a follow-up). Check: no doses, no “you don’t need to go.”

### You do
Track A: log into **their** portal. Find appointments or messages. Do not scroll medical records out loud.  
Track B: run the prompt on **their** real next visit. Print or screenshot the three questions.

**Done when:** they have a portal page open **or** three questions on paper, plus the stop box written down.

### Stop box (on the take-home)
If the AI names a diagnosis, a dose, or “you don’t need to see a doctor,” throw that answer away.

# Lab 9 — Safer money

**Week:** 5 · Fork A · Library / retired  
**Clock:** 35 minutes  
**End state:** They practiced a safer login or bill-pay step **and** they can say they will never share a code.

### One story, one rule
Story: Phone-started fraud still takes the most money per person. The costume is often a bank.  
Rule: **Never move money to protect it. Never read a bank code to a caller.**

### I do
Open a **demo** or screenshot of a bill-pay screen — not a live personal account on the projector. Show where 2FA lives. Show the hang-up rule again.

Optional AI (no account numbers):

> Explain this line from a bill in plain English. I covered the account number: “Late fee after the 15th.”

### We do
Walk the hang-up + call-the-number-on-the-card path with a fake “your account is locked” text.

### You do
On **their** device, one of these:

- Find bill pay or statements and stop before paying anything new, **or**
- Turn on 2FA on the bank if Lab 5 only did email, **or**
- Write the callback number from the back of their card on the take-home.

**Done when:** they have a written bank callback number and they said the code rule out loud.

### Never
Crypto, gold, “guaranteed returns,” or letting AI tell them where to invest.

# Lab 10 — Job or workplace

**Week:** 5 · Fork B (WIOA / job seeker) or Fork C (employer)  
**Clock:** 35 minutes  
**End state:** They finished **one** real task they can show a counselor or a supervisor.

### Fork B — Job search

**Rule:** They edit it. They do not paste a lie.

I do:

> Write one resume bullet for a person who supervised a team of six in a warehouse for 12 years. Plain English. No buzzwords I would not say in an interview.

We do: everyone generates one bullet, then crosses out anything that is not true.

You do: one bullet **or** one interview answer. They read it aloud. They save it.

### Fork C — Workplace

**Rule:** One real tool they already use. Outlook, Teams, Excel, or the CRM. One task.

I do: a **sanitized** example (no customer names): “Summarize this email in three bullets. Draft a two-sentence reply that is polite and asks for a deadline.”

You do: they do that on a real message they are allowed to process. Show a supervisor next week.

**Done when:** a file, a sent draft, or a screenshot of the task exists.

### Safety
No Social Security numbers, no full personnel files, no medical charts into the AI. When in doubt, ask the supervisor what may leave the building.

# Lab 11 — Show the win

**Week:** 6 · Your next chapter  
**Clock:** 15 minutes per person in a 2-minute share + certificates  
**End state:** Post-survey done. One win shown. Certificate in hand.

### One story, one rule
Story: The product was never software. It was dignity plus a skill they can do again.  
Rule: **One next step. Not three pitches.**

### Room plan
1. Post-survey first (same sheet as pre, mark POST).
2. Each person demos or describes **one** win in two minutes — a Lab 1 answer, a call, 2FA, a portal, a resume bullet.
3. Certificates. Group photo only with consent and a signed release.
4. Invite **one** thing: Pioneer Gallery, membership waitlist, or job office hours.

### You do (the 15-minute win)
They pick the lab they are proudest of and run it once more, cold, without you grabbing the phone.

**Done when:** they can do it with you standing still.

### Ops the same day
Outcomes one-pager. Sign-in filed. Testimonials only with a release.

## What we will not add

A twelfth lab. A second AI app in the same week. Coding. How the model works. Crypto. Medical advice. A fourth curriculum for a new buyer.

If a host wants “more,” they get **another cohort**, not another outline.
