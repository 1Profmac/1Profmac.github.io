# 50+TechBridge Free Lessons

**Three free lessons. One promise: you will leave more confident than you arrived.**

These scripts power the public free-lesson path at [learnmoretechnologies.com/start-free-lesson/](https://learnmoretechnologies.com/start-free-lesson/). They are written for adults 50+ and for the people who teach them — Brian McKinney, guest instructors, library partners, and filmed video.

If you only remember one thing about this series, remember the arc:

| Lesson | Title | The job of this lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Welcome | You belong here. You are not too late. Here is the map. |
| 2 | Talk to AI | Ask one real question in one tool and get a useful answer. |
| 3 | Don't Get Scammed | Spot a fake, name a pressure tactic, lock passwords and 2FA. |

That is belong → do → protect. Confidence first. One win second. Safety third. Teaching safety before a person has felt capable is how people freeze. Teaching a tool with no safety is how people get hurt. This sequence is the product.

## Who this is for

Adults 50 and older who are curious, frustrated, or quietly afraid they missed the boat. Many already own a smartphone. Many have used some form of AI without naming it. Almost all have been targeted by a scam call or text.

They are not beginners at life. They are beginners at *this*. Treat them that way.

Two audiences sit in the same room:

- **Working 50–64.** Afraid of being passed over. Need one useful AI win they can use at work this week.
- **Retired 65+.** Afraid of isolation, family distance, and fraud. Need dignity, safety, and a reason to try again.

Same lessons. Different buying reason. Same respect.

## Learning outcomes

After these three lessons, a learner can:

1. Say out loud: “I am allowed to learn this, and I know where to start.”
2. Open one AI tool, ask one real question from their own life, and leave with an answer they can use.
3. Name three pressure tactics, hang up and verify, and turn on a second lock (2FA) on email or banking.

If they can do those three things, the free series worked.

## What we added beyond the original brief

The original request was Lesson 1 Welcome, Lesson 2 Talk to AI, Lesson 3 Don’t Get Scammed — short and long scripts, researched.

Best-in-class also needed the pieces people forget and then pay for later:

- A **research file** so no one quotes a number they cannot defend.
- A **production guide** so every instructor sounds like the same program.
- **Privacy rules** before the first AI prompt (never paste a Social Security number, bank login, or password).
- **How to judge a useful answer** and what to do when AI is confidently wrong.
- **Voice cloning** and a **family code word** — the 2025–2026 threat older adults are actually worried about.
- **Never share a 2FA code** — the hole most password lessons leave open.
- **What to do if money already moved** — freeze, call the bank, report.
- **Official reporting paths:** [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) and [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov).
- A **learner take-home** they can put on the fridge.
- A **series close** so Lesson 3 does not end on fear.
- **Accessibility** as a standard, not an afterthought.
- A note on **Spanish** — we already print Spanish flyers; these scripts should be adapted, not just translated.

## How to film or teach

Read [PRODUCTION-GUIDE.md](PRODUCTION-GUIDE.md) before the first take. The short version:

- Speak about 130–140 words per minute. Pause after every key line.
- Put the takeaway on screen in large type. Never rely on the spoken word alone.
- In Lesson 2, show the real screen. One tool. One question. One useful answer.
- In Lesson 3, use obviously fake examples. Never a real password. Never a real account.
- End every lesson on an action the person can finish today, not a lecture they must remember.
