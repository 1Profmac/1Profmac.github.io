Mission and Artistic Vision

Creative Work — 50 points
"What is your mission or artistic vision, and what do you do to support it?"

Must cover: Your mission + the creative work supporting it  |  Who your work impacts and how they participate  |  Artistic values, traditions, or focus guiding your mission

Learn More Technologies LLC, operating as 50+TechBridge, was honored to receive the City of Austin Nexus Grant in 2024. That award validated our artistic vision: creating pathways for adults 50+ to use AI as a creative tool — for storytelling, writing, design, and full participation in Austin's cultural economy.

Foundation (2024): The Nexus Grant enabled us to test whether this vision was achievable. We brought live, facilitated workshops into Austin's public libraries and cultural centers. Participants — we call them Digital Pioneers — responded powerfully. We proved that adults 50+, including Black and Latino seniors living on fixed incomes and workers facing AI displacement, engage deeply when technology education is treated as a creative act of community-building. We refined the model from real feedback and documented results with signed attendance and session photographs.

Infrastructure (2024–2026): Armed with that validation, we built what scale requires: partnerships across 9 library branches, 3 senior centers, and 13 locations citywide; bilingual English/Spanish curriculum; a creative-output model where participants produce real work; and a methodology grounded in dignity, accessibility, and community. We have now trained 264+ Austin adults.

Scale (Elevate, October 2026–March 2027): This Elevate request funds the essential next phase of work the City already validated: a 6-session AI Literacy Workshop Series culminating in a public Community Showcase of participant-created digital art. We are not asking AACME to fund something new. We are asking AACME to invest in the next required phase of a proven, documented model.

The people we impact are Austin's 50+ adult population, with emphasis on communities most affected by the digital divide. They do not just attend — they write, design, share stories, and build relationships with peers navigating the same challenges.

Our work is guided by three artistic values: dignity (no participant should feel embarrassed for not knowing), accessibility (free programming in English and Spanish at neighborhood locations), and community (creative technology is most powerful when it happens together). The 50+ generation's lived experience is an asset. AI can amplify it rather than replace it. That is what Nexus recognized in 2024. That is what Elevate can help us scale.

Proposed Public Event

Creative Work — 50 points
"What is your proposed public event and how does it reflect the people, places, or cultures of Austin?"

Must cover: When, Where, What, How  |  How it reflects Austin's people, places, and/or cultures  |  "Authentic perspective and meaningful cultural expression"

50+TechBridge proposes a 6-session AI Literacy Workshop Series hosted at Austin Public Library branches and cultural centers, running October 2026 through March 2027. This is not a new experiment. It is the scaled implementation of a model the City of Austin validated through the 2024 Nexus Grant, then strengthened through two years of infrastructure work.

WHEN: Monthly workshops, October 2026 – March 2027, held on weekday mornings and Saturday afternoons to serve both working and retired participants.

WHERE: Austin Public Library branches serving Austin's most culturally diverse neighborhoods, and the Mexican American Cultural Center — trusted gathering places that have served these communities for generations.

WHAT: 90-minute hands-on workshops teaching practical AI as a creative tool — conversational assistants, AI-powered writing and design, digital storytelling, scam protection, and everyday creative tasks: letters, memoirs, health information, family connection, and small-business expression.

HOW: Groups of 15–20, facilitated by trained instructors, with printed take-home guides in English and Spanish. Every session includes practice on participant-owned devices, peer sharing, and time for questions. The series culminates in a public Community Showcase of participant-created digital work.

Foundation to scale: In 2024 we tested this format. From 2024–2026 we expanded it to 9 libraries, 3 senior centers, and 13 locations, training 264+ Digital Pioneers. Elevate funds the next planned phase: a concentrated public series with bilingual materials, deeper East Austin and Latino community reach, and a culminating arts event.

This program reflects Austin authentically. We meet people in their own neighborhoods — in libraries that have served Austin communities for generations. Our curriculum centers Austin's own stories: a grandmother learning to video call her grandchildren, a retired educator writing her memoir with AI assistance, a 62-year-old worker preparing for a job market that has changed around him. These are Austin stories. This is Austin culture — resilient, community-rooted, and forward-looking. Elevate lets us fulfill the promise Nexus started.

Marketing Plan

Public Connection — 20 points
"How will you market your proposed event?"

Must cover: Methods to reach residents, visitors, and tourists  |  How you promote Austin as a cultural destination  |  How people will feel connected and encouraged to attend

Our marketing plan is built on channels we already use — not a speculative outreach strategy. Nexus-funded sessions in 2024 taught us that adults 50+ do not find programs primarily on Instagram. They find them through trusted neighborhood institutions, printed materials in their language, and a personal invitation from someone they already know.

From 2024–2026 we formalized those channels. They filled 264+ seats across 13 locations. Elevate lets us run the same proven mix at series scale.

Channels: (1) Austin Public Library network — posters, table tents, and staff referrals at branches citywide. (2) Mexican American Cultural Center — flyers and bilingual postcards distributed through their existing community networks. (3) Agentic50 email newsletter — weekly AI programming updates reaching 50+ adults and allied organizations throughout Austin. (4) LinkedIn and Facebook — organic and boosted content targeting Austin's 50+ professional and community audiences. (5) Printed 4x6 bilingual postcards — distributed at senior centers, community centers, faith communities, and healthcare waiting rooms across Austin. (6) AARP Austin chapter — program listing in their community calendar and member communications.

Promoting Austin as a cultural destination: Our workshops are hosted at Austin's most culturally significant community anchors — the very spaces that define Austin's identity as a city that honors its diverse creative traditions. Programming at these locations reinforces Austin's reputation as a place where technology and cultural legacy coexist, and where older adults are active participants in the city's future — not bystanders to it.

Why people will attend: Our marketing speaks directly, in participants' own language: "You belong here. You're not too old. This is for you." Every piece of outreach uses real language from real Austin adults. That authenticity, combined with trusted neighborhood locations and zero cost to attend, is the draw we already proved works.
