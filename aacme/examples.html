Foundational Evolution

Section-by-section examples for the 2027 Elevate application. The story is not “fund something new.” It is: Nexus validated the foundation in 2024, 2024–2026 built the infrastructure, Elevate funds the next required phase of a plan already in motion.

The story every section should tell

  1. Opening: We were honored to receive the City of Austin Nexus Grant in 2024. This application continues that partnership at scale.
  2. Middle: Here is what we learned in 2024. Here is what we built in 2024–2026. Here is what we are ready to accomplish in the Elevate window (October 2026–March 2027).
  3. Closing: The City’s initial investment proved the vision works. This Elevate investment fulfills the promise we started together.

1

Executive Summary / Cover Letter

Not an official Elevate field — use this tone in every answer
If a reviewer reads only the first paragraph of Q1, they should already know this is a continuation, not a first ask.
Weak version (typical)

“Learn More Technologies is seeking $25,500 in AACME 2027 Elevate funding to expand our technology training program for underserved adults. Our program has been successful and we believe Elevate funding will help us serve more people.”

Problem: Generic, no credibility markers, sounds like every other applicant.

Strong version (Foundational Evolution)

Learn More Technologies was honored to receive the City of Austin Nexus Grant in 2024, enabling us to lay critical foundational work. We tested our model, validated our approach, and proved the concept works. We have now spent 2024–2026 building the infrastructure to scale responsibly. This AACME 2027 Elevate application requests $25,500 for the essential next phase of a strategic plan we started in 2024: taking our proven 50+TechBridge model from current capacity to a public 6-session series (October 2026–March 2027) culminating in a Community Showcase.

We are not asking AACME to fund something new. We are asking them to invest in the next required phase of something the City has already validated and we have already proven successful.

Why this works
  • Opens with credibility (the City already funded you)
  • Shows intentional strategy (not reactive growth)
  • Demonstrates learning (foundation → infrastructure → scale)
  • Positions Elevate as logical progression
  • Respectful but confident tone

Artistic Vision / Mission Alignment

Maps to official Q1 — Mission and Artistic Vision · 50 pts · 3,000 characters
“What is your mission or artistic vision, and what do you do to support it?”

Must cover: Mission + the creative work supporting it  |  Who the work impacts and how they participate  |  Artistic values, traditions, or focus guiding the mission

Answer using Foundational Evolution

Our vision — to create pathways for overlooked talent to access technology as a creative tool, with dignity and economic security — was validated by the City of Austin Nexus Grant in 2024.

Foundation (2024)

The Nexus Award enabled us to test whether this vision was achievable. We served our first Digital Pioneer cohorts in Austin libraries and cultural centers, refined the model from real feedback, and proved that adults 50+, career-changers, and overlooked talent respond powerfully to genuine access. Documentation: signed attendance and session photographs.

Infrastructure (2024–2026)

Armed with 2024 validation, we invested in what scale requires: formalized community partnerships (9 library branches, 3 senior centers, 13 locations), bilingual English/Spanish curriculum, a creative-output model, and free delivery at neighborhood sites. We grew to 264+ participants trained.

Scale (Elevate: Oct 2026–Mar 2027)

This Elevate investment is the essential next phase: a 6-session public series reaching ~150 adults 50+, 10 Spanish-language sessions, 75 participants producing creative output, and a Community Showcase for 60+ attendees.

What makes this culturally important: Austin’s economic and cultural future depends on including all workers and all storytellers. We are building a model proving that age and background are not barriers — they are assets. Our approach challenges systemic bias while meeting real creative and economic need. That is what the City already recognized in 2024. This is what Elevate can help us scale.

Copy-ready Q1 (trimmed to the 3,000-character limit, with required subpoints) lives on the application narratives page.

Community Impact & Cultural Relevance

Maps to official Q2 — Proposed Public Event · 50 pts · 3,000 characters
Describe the community you serve, how the project reflects their values and experiences, and how they will benefit.
Answer using Foundational Evolution

Our community: adults 50+ in Austin, career-changers, workers displaced by automation, and underserved populations — especially Black and Latino adults — locked out of tech and creative-technology opportunity.

Foundation year revealed their true needs (2024)

Through Nexus-funded programming, we discovered that this community does not just want job training — they want dignity, visibility, and proof that their decades of experience matter in a tech-first world. They want instructors who respect their intelligence. They want small, patient groups, not factory models. They want community, not isolation.

One 2024 participant told us: [Add a real quote about transformation before submitting.]

Infrastructure built with community input (2024–2026)

We did not scale blindly. We embedded feedback loops. We formalized partnerships with Austin Public Library branches, senior centers, and cultural sites including outreach to the Mexican American Cultural Center. We created free programming reflecting economic reality. We adjusted scheduling to weekday mornings and Saturday afternoons. We built bilingual materials.

The result: 264+ community members served across 13 locations, with sign-in documentation on file.

Community benefits from Elevate (Oct 2026–Mar 2027)

  • Reach ~150 adults 50+ through a concentrated 6-session public series
  • Deepen partnerships with Austin Public Library and the Mexican American Cultural Center
  • Deliver 10 Spanish-language sessions and fully bilingual take-home guides
  • Support 75 participants in producing creative output
  • Host a public Community Showcase (60+ attendees) as both celebration and cultural event

Cultural relevance

We directly counter narratives that say adults 50+ are obsolete. We prove that overlooked talent, when genuinely supported, becomes valuable, stable, and grateful creative-technology participants. That is culturally important for Austin’s identity and economy.

Program Description

Maps to official Q2 — Proposed Public Event (When / Where / What / How)
Describe in detail the proposed project. Explain what you will do, when it will happen, and how it will work.
Project: Scaled implementation of a proven model (Oct 2026–Mar 2027)

Foundation (what we started in 2024)

Core model, validated through the Nexus Grant:

  • Live, facilitated AI literacy workshops in public libraries and cultural centers
  • Small groups for personalized attention
  • Current practitioners as instructors
  • Flexible scheduling accommodating real life
  • Community partnerships ensuring culturally aware support
  • Creative output — participants make things, not just take notes

Infrastructure (what we built in 2024–2026)

  • Curriculum aligned with participant need and creative-technology practice
  • Partnerships across 9 library branches, 3 senior centers, 13 locations
  • Free programming (100% of learners — no tuition barrier)
  • Bilingual English/Spanish materials
  • Data collection: sign-in sheets, photos, testimonials

Results to date

  • 264+ participants trained (2024–2026)
  • 13 locations active
  • Nexus Grant ($5,000) delivered on time and within budget

Proposed Elevate implementation

WHEN: October 2026–March 2027, weekday mornings and Saturday afternoons.

WHERE: Austin Public Library branches serving culturally diverse neighborhoods, plus the Mexican American Cultural Center.

WHAT: 6-session, 90-minute AI Literacy Workshop Series — conversational AI, AI-powered writing and creativity, scam protection, everyday creative tasks — culminating in a public Community Showcase.

HOW: Groups of 15–20, trained instructors, printed take-home guides in English and Spanish, practice on participant-owned devices.

Why this specific scale

Not arbitrary. 2024–2026 infrastructure work showed that the current team can run a 6-session public series; library and cultural partners can absorb this growth; bilingual materials are required; and a Showcase is the right public-facing close. We are scaling deliberately, not recklessly.

Activity window reminder: Official Elevate activities are October 2026–March 2027. Do not describe the proposed project as a 2027–2029 multi-year scale-up in the official answers. Keep longer-term 2027–2029 vision in sustainability language only.

Budget & Sustainability

Maps to official Q4 — Budget · 30 pts
“What is your proposed project budget?” Complete the table and explain how it supports the project.
Budget alignment

2024 Nexus Grant ($5,000): Used to test the model and build the foundation. First library-based cohorts, curriculum prototypes, documentation systems.

2024–2026 infrastructure development: Refined the model from 2024 learning. Served 264+ participants. Cost per session decreased as partnerships and curriculum amortized. Built sustainability infrastructure (library venue in-kind, bilingual materials, instructor methodology).

2026–2027 Elevate budget ($25,500): Scaled implementation at proven cost — 6 public sessions, bilingual materials, marketing through channels already tested, Community Showcase, administration at Nexus reporting standard. Total project value ~$42,500 with leverage.

Every budget line item is justified by 2024–2026 learning. We are not guessing at costs. We are budgeting based on what we have actually spent at each phase of development.

Sustainability beyond Elevate

  • Diversified revenue (not AACME-dependent): employer / cohort training contracts via Work With Us; sliding-scale or sponsored B2B delivery; foundation grants outside AACME; 501(c)(3) in process for a future Elevate Nonprofit-tier path.
  • Infrastructure already built: library venue in-kind; founder uncompensated time (~$8,000 in the grant period); documented curriculum that is repeatable.
  • Scalable model design: curriculum is repeatable; partnership infrastructure is replicable; Showcase format is reusable.

We are using this Elevate investment to build toward independence — not to create permanent dependence on a single City source.

Evaluation & Outcomes

Not an official Elevate field — use these numbers inside Q1–Q3 and internal reporting
How will you measure success? What metrics will you track?
Evaluation framework built on 2024–2026 learning

2024 (foundation) — what we measured

  • Participants served and documented (sign-in sheets + photos)
  • Sites activated (libraries, senior centers)
  • Nexus deliverables completed on time

2024–2026 (infrastructure) outcomes

  • 264+ participants trained
  • 9 library branches, 3 senior centers, 13 locations
  • Bilingual materials in use

These are not aspirational. These are proven.

Elevate (Oct 2026–Mar 2027) targets

  • 150 adults 50+ served
  • 10–12 active delivery sites
  • 20–24 training cohorts / 6 featured public sessions in the series
  • 100 completing a full track (~65%)
  • 75 producing creative output
  • 10 Spanish-language sessions
  • 85%+ participant satisfaction
  • 60+ Community Showcase attendees

Qualitative metrics

  • Community feedback on cultural relevance (session surveys)
  • Partner feedback from library and cultural-center staff
  • Graduate follow-up where contact info is provided

We are not just counting bodies. We are tracking transformation. Elevate funding enables us to prove the model works at public-series scale.

Do not invent placement rates, salary increases, or satisfaction scores of “X/5” unless you have the source data in hand. Use documented counts (264+, 13 locations, Nexus $5,000) and named Elevate targets (150 served, 85%+ satisfaction) instead.

Marketing Plan

Official Q5 — Public Connection · 20 pts · 3,000 characters
“How will you market your proposed event?”

Must cover: Methods to reach residents, visitors, and tourists  |  How you promote Austin as a cultural destination  |  How people will feel connected and encouraged to attend

Answer using Foundational Evolution

Our marketing plan is built on channels we already use — not a speculative outreach strategy.

Foundation (2024)

Nexus-funded sessions taught us that adults 50+ do not find programs primarily on Instagram. They find them through trusted neighborhood institutions, printed materials in their language, and a personal invitation from someone they already know.

Infrastructure (2024–2026)

We formalized those channels: Austin Public Library posters and staff referrals; bilingual 4×6 postcards at senior centers, faith communities, and healthcare waiting rooms; the Agentic50 newsletter; AARP Austin chapter listings; and organic LinkedIn and Facebook content. These methods filled 264+ seats across 13 locations.

Elevate implementation

  • Austin Public Library network — posters, table tents, and staff referrals
  • Mexican American Cultural Center — bilingual flyers and postcards
  • Agentic50 email newsletter
  • LinkedIn and Facebook, organic and boosted
  • Printed bilingual postcards at senior centers, community centers, faith communities, and healthcare waiting rooms
  • AARP Austin chapter calendar and member communications

Austin as a cultural destination: Workshops sit inside Austin’s culturally significant community anchors — the spaces that define Austin as a city that honors diverse creative traditions. Programming there reinforces Austin’s reputation as a place where technology and cultural legacy coexist, and where older adults are participants in the city’s future, not bystanders to it.

Why people will attend: Marketing speaks in participants’ own language: “You belong here. You’re not too old. This is for you.” Authenticity, trusted neighborhood locations, and zero cost — the draw we already proved works.

Toolkit

Language patterns to use throughout

  • “Built on the foundation established in 2024 through the City of Austin Nexus Grant…”
  • “Our 2024–2026 infrastructure development taught us that…”
  • “This Elevate investment funds the essential next phase of strategic work we started in 2024…”
  • “We proved the model works in 2024. We built the infrastructure in 2024–2026. Elevate enables scale in October 2026–March 2027…”
  • “Every budget decision is informed by 2024–2026 learning…”
  • “Our team has direct experience managing City of Austin funds with demonstrated accountability…”
  • “This was always the plan: foundation, infrastructure, scale…”

What not to say

  • “We’re a new organization seeking first funding.” You received Nexus in 2024 — you are established.
  • “We want to expand into new areas.” You are scaling proven work, not experimenting.
  • “We need help figuring out our model.” You figured it out in 2024 — now you are executing.
  • “Please take a chance on us.” The City already took that chance in 2024 — you are asking for continued partnership.
  • “Lexus Nexus Award.” Official name in our files and City records: City of Austin Nexus Grant ($5,000). Use that name in the submitted application.
  • Invented placement %, salary lift, or “X/5 satisfaction” unless the source data is on file. Reviewers can ask for it.

Throughout: one test for every paragraph

  1. Does this reflect the foundation the City validated in 2024?
  2. Does it show what we built in 2024–2026, not just what we hope to build?
  3. Does Elevate read as the next required phase — not a first experiment?
