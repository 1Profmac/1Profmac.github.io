1. Is this in line with projected future needs?

Yes. The demographic, the labor market, and the AgeTech category all move toward LMT’s customer — with one important caveat: most AgeTech capital is chasing clinical care, not workforce training. That is a funding-mix problem, not a demand problem.

What the next three years actually require
  • More 50–74 year-olds will still be working. BLS projects U.S. labor-force participation for ages 55–64 to rise from 65.9% (2024) to 68.6% (2034), and for 65–74 from 27.1% to 29.6%. The workforce is getting older, not younger. BLS Employment Projections Table 3.3
  • AI is already pushing 55+ workers out of exposed jobs. Post-ChatGPT, workers 55+ in AI-exposed occupations leave work more often (unemployment and “voluntary” exits). Upskilling is no longer a nice-to-have; it is how this cohort stays employed. Center for Retirement Research / Sanzenbacher 2026; CNBC Jul 2026
  • Ageism is a priced cost, not a slogan. $850B/year (AARP & Oxford Economics). $18K per worker (LSE & Protiviti). Employers and boards can put a number on inaction.
  • AgeTech is a $286B global market in 2026, growing ~10.4% real CAGR through 2031. Lifelong learning is a large established segment; fraud-protection and cognitive tools are the faster growers (15–17%). LMT sits at learning + fraud (Week 2 scams) + workforce — not in wearables. SilverEconomy AgeTech Market Report 2026
  • Where the money is concentrating: AI-in-aging-and-elderly-care is forecast ~$68B (2026) → ~$158B (2030), ~24% CAGR. That is clinical/care tech. LMT should not try to become that company. It should sell training, documentation, and trusted access to the institutions that already buy those outcomes (boards, employers, libraries, insurers). The Business Research Company / Research and Markets 2026

Funder one-liner: The 50+ adult is not a niche. It is the labor-force story of 2027–2029. Almost nobody is selling WIOA-eligible, in-person AI training designed for that adult. That gap is the company.

2. Four concepts — score the bet

Compare LMT’s Pioneer Model with the three other ways capital usually “does 50+.” Scores are for this founder, this footprint, a 36-month profit clock — not for who wins the decade.

Why A beats B even though B is bigger: GetSetUp proved demand and proved that scale without priced institutional SKUs still does not print profit. LMT is smaller and should stay smaller-on-purpose until ETPL + retainers exist. The Pioneer Model is GetSetUp’s lesson applied early: do not put the P&L on the 50+ learner.

3. Three-year profit potential — Pioneer Model only

Grants (Elevate, Nexus-class) are restricted fuel, not operating profit. Figures are contribution after delivery cost (facilitator, materials, travel, software) and before paying Brian a market CEO salary. Texas ITA caps are set by local boards (commonly roughly $7k–$9k per participant in many regions; confirm per board) — cohort pricing must sit inside that envelope once ETPL is live.

4. Strategy — 36 months if we stay the course
FY27 — Unlock the buyer (months 1–12)
  1. Unify public prices to ITA-range SKUs: Pilot / Cohort / Annual. Kill $500 and $1,500 as public headline prices.
  2. Submit / confirm ETPL. SAM.gov + UEI. One-page capability statement. Named facilitator (even part-time).
  3. Sell the 90-minute pilot weekly. Convert 1 in 3 pilots to a 6-week cohort.
  4. Two proposal conversations per week with boards, SHRM/HR, library systems, and one healthcare or insurer for a future Barb conversation — not a build.
  5. AGENTIC50 guest pattern: Pioneer → Buyer → Peer. Book 3 buyer guests who chair rooms you need.
  6. Gallery Oct–Dec 2026 as proof, not as the P&L. File one outcomes one-pager.
FY28 — Stop being a solo delivery business (months 13–24)
  1. Facilitator runs community cohorts. Brian runs employer/WIOA and keynotes only.
  2. Site-license the kit to 2–3 additional locations (train-the-trainer).
  3. One Barb white-label pilot (library or healthcare) — no new product, a contract on what already exists.
  4. 501(c)(3), if approved, funds free seats. The LLC still invoices institutions.
FY29 — License + stage (months 25–36)
  1. Repeat retainers. Out-of-region licenses. Keynote floor held at $5k+.
  2. Do not launch a consumer membership as the center of gravity. $27/mo is a sidecar after B2B is booked.
5. Hard pivot vs stay the course — the clock

Do not pivot off adults 50+. Pivot the offer and the payer if the clock below trips. Dates are from September 2026.

Stay the course when all of these are true
  • At least one paid institutional invoice (employer, board, library system, or sponsor) by month 9.
  • ETPL submitted by month 6; in conversation with a board by month 12.
  • A named facilitator exists (even contract) by month 6.
  • Public prices no longer conflict; ITA-range SKUs are the only list.
  • Pilot → cohort conversion is happening (even 1 of 4).
  • Speaking inquiries include a next-step cohort, not only a $500 talk.
Hard-pivot triggers — do not wait for “one more grant”
  • Month 12: Zero paid institutional contracts AND ETPL not submitted. The concept is untested, not “early.”
  • Month 18: ETPL rejected twice and no employer/library paid program. Boards will not be the engine. Pivot payer to employer retainers + site licenses only, or to sponsored library series — not to consumer courses.
  • Month 18: Brian is still 100% of delivery. The company cannot be funded; it is a practice. Hire, license, or stop claiming scale.
  • Any time: Consumer membership or affiliate “AI side hustle” exceeds ~25% of revenue. That is a different, worse company. Kill it.
  • Do not: Pivot into clinical AgeTech, adtech, or “we run your ads.” Wrong assets.

Allowed “soft pivots” (not concept changes): (1) If WIOA is slow, overweight employer/HR and library-system contracts. (2) If in-person stalls, overweight site license + Barb. (3) If Texas boards stall, take the same SKU to another state’s board that already lists digital-literacy ITAs. The customer is still 50+. The invoice is still an institution.

6. What to tell a funder in 90 seconds
  1. The need is not speculative. Older participation is rising, AI is ejecting 55+ workers from exposed jobs, and ageism is a $850B / $18K-per-worker cost.
  2. Most AgeTech money is in care tech. We are not that. We sell WIOA-eligible, MBE-certified AI training and proof, delivered at 9 in-person central Texas locations (6 APL branches, AARP-affiliated senior centers, Foundation Communities veterans program).
  3. Compared with GetSetUp-class platforms, philanthropy literacy, and clinical AgeTech, this is the only concept that matches this founder’s assets and can produce profit in 36 months without a hardware stack.
  4. Base case: low-six-figures revenue by FY29, contribution in the $90–150k year if ETPL + one facilitator land. Upside is licensing. Downside is a teaching practice.
  5. Use of funds: facilitator, ETPL/SAM, capability statement, 90 days of sales. Not a new app.
  6. Kill criteria are dated. Month 12 without an institutional invoice, or month 18 still solo-delivered, we change the payer mix or we stop calling it a company.

Recommendation: Stay the course on the 50+ demographic and the Pioneer Model. Raise against FY27 unlocks (ETPL, facilitator, first paid cohort). Re-forecast at month 12 with invoices in hand. Do not raise against a clinical AgeTech story or a consumer-app story.
