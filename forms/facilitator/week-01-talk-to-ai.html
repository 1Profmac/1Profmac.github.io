Week 1 Guide

End state: Every person asked one real question in one AI tool and got a useful answer. If they can do that, Week 1 succeeded. Device setup is only the first 10 minutes — not the lesson.

Video scripts: SCRIPT-lesson-02-talk-to-ai.txt. Why the lines changed: week-01-talk-to-ai-research.html (Pew, AARP, Senior Planet, socials).

Before you walk in
  • Pick one tool for the whole cohort (ChatGPT default; Gemini or Copilot only if the room is already on that stack — not all three).
  • Create or confirm a classroom account if Wi‑Fi login will be a barrier. On chatgpt.com, look for Stay logged out / Continue without an account.
  • Print: sign-in, 3–5 photo releases, take-home cards, pre-survey (first session only).
  • Write 3 demo questions on a sticky: a recipe or letter, a trip, a work/life task. Have one working-adult example and one retired-adult example.
Room plan
BlockSay / do
Welcome + device (10)Pre-survey. Goal: family / health / job / work. Font size. Open the browser. “We are not learning the phone today. We are getting an answer.”
One story, one rule (10)Story: you are not late — most people your age have never done this. Bridge: Siri / Alexa / Maps is a little AI; this is a conversation. Rule: the AI can be wrong — you decide if the answer is useful. Do not type SSN, bank numbers, or passwords.
I do (15)Open chatgpt.com. Show Stay logged out if a login box appears. Type the starter prompt. Read the answer out loud. Change one word and show it update. Point at the microphone: “You can speak this if typing is hard.”
We do (25)Everyone uses the same starter prompt together. Wait for every screen. Help one person at a time. Do not introduce a second tool.
You do (30)They ask their question (insurance or HOA letter, job task, recipe, birthday note, appointment). Then one follow-up: “Make that shorter” or “What should I check with a real person?” Win = they can read the answer back.
Save + exit (20)Fill the take-home card. Exit slip. Next week: scams and fake voices. Confirm date.
Starter prompt (write on the board)

I’m over 50 and new to AI. I want to use it for something practical in my life. Explain the most useful thing I can do with it today, in 5 short steps, in plain language. Do not give medical or legal advice.

Say this comparison once (then stop)

Google hands you a stack of websites. This tool sits down and talks it through with you. If they already use Siri or Alexa: “Same idea. This one can hold a real conversation.” Do not demo Gemini and Copilot in the same hour.

If someone is stuck
  • No email yet → Stay logged out, pair them, or use the classroom account.
  • Tiny text → Settings → Display → largest font. Then come back to the tool.
  • Afraid to type → they dictate, or they tap the microphone. You type the first line only if needed; they do the rest.
  • Wants a different app → “One tool this week. You can try another after class.”

Do not teach how the model works, compare five chatbots, make a medication diagnosis the demo, or let Week 1 become a smartphone class. If they leave with one useful answer, you are on brand and you can sell this as a 90-minute pilot.
