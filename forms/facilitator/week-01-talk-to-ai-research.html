Research

Why this file exists. Lesson 2 is the product. Every line in the HeyGen script should either reduce shame, get them into one tool, or produce one useful answer. This sheet records what Facebook, Reddit, YouTube, Quora, LinkedIn, AARP, Pew, and competitor classes actually say — and what we changed in the script because of it.

Keep teaching one tool. Senior Planet’s national class is a lecture covering ChatGPT and Gemini, plus privacy. Our edge is local, hands-on, one tool, one win. Do not add Copilot or Gemini in Week 1.
What we changed in the video script — and why
AngleWhat the socials / research saidWhat Barb now says
You are not late Quora’s most-asked 50+ question is still “Is it too late to learn AI at 60?” Pew (Feb 2026): 77% of adults 65+ never use chatbots; 58% of 50–64 never do. Substack/retiree writers: “You are not behind. You are in the majority.” “About three out of four adults 65 and older have never used a chatbot. You are not behind. You are right on time.”
You already use a little AI AARP: about half of 50+ use or want a voice assistant (Siri/Alexa). Family how-tos: “It’s like Alexa, but it can hold a real conversation.” Facebook/YouTube comments treat ChatGPT as a mysterious new species. Bridging from Siri lowers the leap. Siri, Alexa, Google Maps, Netflix suggestions = a small piece of the same idea. This one can hold a conversation.
Google vs conversation Dr. Margaret Sass (Boise State senior workshops): “Seniors get frustrated having to click through a dozen websites to find one answer. ChatGPT just gives you what you need right away.” Senior blogs now teach an explicit Google vs ChatGPT table. “Google hands you a stack of websites… This tool sits down and talks it through with you.”
Stay logged out OpenAI Help Center: try ChatGPT at chatgpt.com before creating an account. UI copy is often Stay logged out, not only “continue without an account.” Guest chats are not saved. Look for Stay logged out, or Continue without an account. Free account later if you want to save chats.
Voice / microphone Family teaching guides and senior TikToks: typing on glass is the #1 friction. ChatGPT voice/mic is the Siri-like on-ramp. AARP interest in voice assistants is already high. “If typing feels hard, look for a little microphone… You can speak your question instead.”
Privacy in the same lesson Pew non-users: 79% cite concern about how personal information will be used. 76% don’t trust accuracy. AARP: 9 in 10 older adults worry about AI-powered scams. Do not wait until Week 2 for “don’t paste your SSN.” One line in 2a: no Social Security number, bank numbers, or passwords. Full scams lesson still Week 2.
Starter prompt is 50+, not 65 Two audiences on LinkedIn/Reddit: retired 65+ (family gap, isolation) and working 50–62 (job/AI training gap). AARP: 49% of workers 50+ want AI training; only 12% get it. Employed 50+ are twice as likely to have used gen AI (42% vs 21%). “I am over 50 and new to AI…” plus “add a letter, a trip, a recipe, or a job task.”
Insurance letter, not a meds diagnosis Socials and senior blogs: first wins are letters, recipes, HOA/insurance in plain English, birthday notes, trip plans. Health is a top interest and a top risk. AARP: they want AI to make life manageable, not replace a human. Keep “ask your doctor” — don’t make a medication explanation the hero story. Hero story: 72-year-old, Austin library, insurance letter in plain English. Follow-up line: “What should I check with a real person before I use this?”
Human stays in charge AARP job-of-AI survey: high interest in translation, simplifying info, home monitoring; pull-back when AI replaces a human. Dr. Sass: AI should never replace human interaction. Don’t sell companionship as the product. “It is extra help. It is not a replacement for your doctor, your family, or a real friend.”
What we kept (already matched the socials)
  • “That was it?” — same reaction Dr. Sass reports: fear → curiosity the moment they see a real answer.
  • Conversation partner + taxi-driver context — Senior Planet’s own prompt drill is Task / Context / Format. Specificity is the whole game.
  • Follow-up as the power skill — every competitor guide now teaches “make it shorter / explain simply.” We already had this. We added one verification follow-up.
  • No wrong question / doesn’t judge you — Quora/Facebook pain phrase: “I feel stupid when I try.” Shame is the barrier, not IQ.
  • One tool, chatgpt.com, free first session — still correct. ChatGPT is the #1 chatbot in every age group (Pew 2026).

Socials — pain phrases and first-win language
FacebookRedditYouTube QuoraLinkedInAARP forums
  • Facebook / retirement groups: “This morning I went to a free workshop learning about Chat GPT… What you can do with it will blow your mind… especially people who are retired.” First-win posts are letters, recipes, trip plans — not “what is a large language model.”
  • Reddit / Quora: “Is it too late at 60?” “I feel a bit foolish.” Ageism thread: “they’re just lazy.” Success stories: 67-year-old teacher writing children’s stories; 60-year-old consultant adding AI to his rates. Core fear is permission, not steps.
  • YouTube (Mittler Senior Technology and similar): Nobody said “I learned a new skill.” They said “changed my life,” “just having someone explain these things,” “narrowed the generation gap.” Product = confidence and reconnection.
  • LinkedIn: 55+ workers leaving AI-exposed jobs; 49% want training / 12% get it; LinkedIn Learning age gap collapsed from 13.5 points to 1.6 when access exists. Working 50+ need a job-task example, not only grandkids.
  • AARP community: “Befuddled after a software update.” Trust, privacy, and ease of use beat “how the model works.” 3 in 5 say tech is not designed with their age in mind.
