Monetization, program design, and the gap between the current course and the new spine

This packet is the printed record of the Learn More Technologies strategy conversations: who should pay, what the Digital Pioneer Core should contain, and what to change in the existing 6-week course. It is a planning document, not a learner workbook.

1. Monetization — who pays, and for what

The money is not in selling courses to adults 50+. It is in selling access, proof, and delivery to organizations that need that audience: employers, workforce boards, libraries, and brands.

Learn More Technologies already has the hard parts: Austin delivery footprint, a free WIOA-aligned curriculum, MBE certification, a founder story, and a clear market claim ($76T wealth, almost no ad spend). What is missing is a paid product ladder that matches that positioning. Public prices ($500 keynotes, $1,500 cohorts, “from $5,000” brand work) sit below the value of the problem described on the site.

Keep the flagship course free to learners. Charge the institutions that need completions, certificates, and a community they cannot fake.

Strategies that fit this business
  1. Make WIOA / ETPL the revenue engine. Finish ETPL or equivalent board contracting. Sell a packaged ITA offer: workshop, multi-session cohort, and documentation bundle. Compete on completion rate, 50+ design, and bilingual delivery — not on cheap.
  2. Stop selling $1,500 “cohorts.” Sell an annual workforce program. Example SKUs: 90-minute pilot; standard cohort $7,500–$15,000; multi-site / quarterly retainers $25,000–$60,000 / year; branded portal as an add-on.
  3. White-label / site-license the curriculum. Facilitator kit, slides, workbooks, completion tracking, train-the-trainer. Site fee + per-learner or annual license. This is how a one-person delivery model becomes a company.
  4. Brand advisory as a retainer, not a one-off audit. “From $5,000” is an entry audit. The real product is ongoing 50+ fluency plus paid, consented Pioneer research access. Use MBE in RFPs, not only on the homepage.
  5. Sponsored community programs, not ads on the free course. Banks, hospitals, insurers, retailers pay to be the named sponsor of a library series. Sponsor the program, not the lesson. One bad affiliate would wreck trust.
  6. Launch Digital Pioneer Membership as a sidecar. $27/month is the right consumer price and the wrong center of gravity. Cap founder time. The free course remains the funnel.
  7. Raise speaking fees; treat the stage as pipeline. Floor around $5,000–$10,000 plus travel. Every keynote has a next step: diagnostic, cohort, or brand briefing.
  8. Grants as fuel, not identity. Use philanthropy to cover free community seats. Contracts pay the company.
  9. Productize proof. Annual 50+ AI Readiness / Brand Ageism report, a short paid agency briefing, and a standard outcomes one-pager for procurement.

Do not

  • Put the P&L on course sales to individual 50+ learners
  • Affiliate-sell gold, crypto, or “AI side hustles”
  • Keep three public price lists ($500 vs $1,500 vs $2,500 / $5,000)
  • Scale by cloning Brian — scale by licensing + facilitators
  • Pitch “we run your ads”

Do next

  • One public offer sheet with three paid SKUs and one free funnel
  • ETPL / workforce-board contracting in Texas
  • Sell the annual program, not the $1,500 pilot as the end state
  • Train-the-trainer so library partners can run without Brian in the room
  • Open membership only after the B2B calendar is booked

Positioning: Institutions pay for outcomes. Sponsors pay for trusted access. Learners get a free start and an optional membership. The $76 trillion argument only works if the invoice matches it.

Live session script — 110 minutes
  1. Arrive and settle (10) — names, what worked last week, devices on
  2. One story, one rule (10) — why this matters at 50+; no theory dump
  3. I do (15) — facilitator demos slowly on a big screen
  4. We do (25) — class does the same steps together
  5. You do (30) — they complete it on their phone or laptop
  6. Save the win (10) — write the steps on a one-page take-home card
  7. Confidence check (10) — 3-question exit slip + next week preview

Self-paced version of the same module: 6–8 minute captioned video (EN/ES), click-along checklist, one practice task, “did it work?” checkbox, optional office-hours replay. Always include a printed one-pager, an 8-word glossary, a “stop if this happens” box, and a WIOA attendance checkbox.

Program wrapper — what boards and HR buy
  1. Intake: device type, language (EN/ES), goal (family / health / job / work)
  2. Pre-survey: confidence, scam awareness, one “I cannot do this yet” item
  3. 6 sessions + makeup path (missed class = self-paced lesson + 15-minute check-in)
  4. Post-survey + certificate (name, hours, modules, date)
  5. Outcomes one-pager for the host (attendance, confidence change, 3 quotes)
  6. Pioneer next step: gallery nomination, membership, or job-search office hours

Pedagogy rules

  • One tool per module
  • Their device, not a lab PC
  • Same 5 steps, three times
  • Shame-free language — never “even grandma”
  • Verify, don’t worship the model
  • Bilingual by design
  • Peer facilitator script so Brian is not the only teacher
  • ~18 lessons total, not 40

Leave out of the core

  • How the internet or LLMs work
  • Coding or “become an AI engineer”
  • Ten apps in one day
  • Crypto, gold, or side-hustle schemes
  • Medical or legal advice from the AI
  • A fourth curriculum per buyer

Monetization fit of the course: Free self-paced unlocks Modules 0–2. Certificate + Modules 3–6 run in a cohort or membership. Employer SKU = Core + Workplace AI fork + outcomes report. WIOA SKU = Core + Job Search fork + attendance/ITA packet. If you only build one thing next: six identical module templates, one week-5 fork, and the session script.

3. Gap analysis — current course vs new spine

You already have the container right (6 weeks, 12 hours, peer facilitator, libraries). What does not match is the inside of the weeks — and two different courses are being described in public.

Partner / GitHub pages still sell: smartphones, internet safety, telehealth, video calling, online services. learnmoretechnologies.com sells: AI Fundamentals, Career & Job Search, Finance & Safety, Health / Independence / Business. The new structure is how you make them one.

The suggestion is a rewrite of week titles and labs, not a new organization.

What to change
  1. Pick one public outline. Publish the six Pioneer modules everywhere.
  2. Move “how to use a phone” out of Week 1. Keep 10–15 minutes of device setup inside every session. A whole week on the phone keeps employers and advanced 50+ learners out.
  3. Make Week 1 an AI win. End state: they asked one real question and got a useful answer.
  4. Upgrade Week 2 to AI-era scams. Keep passwords and 2FA. Add fake grandchild voice, fake photos, fake invoices.
  5. Give Week 5 a fork. Three labs, one per cohort type: banking; resume/interview; workplace tools.
  6. Turn Week 6 into a finish line. One personal project, certificate, optional Pioneer story. AACME already wants a showcase — this is that, every cohort.
  7. Write one session script and use it every week. Instructor package is strong on ops, thin on teaching.
  8. Add a 10-minute Welcome / pre-survey. Lock a goal and a confidence score. WIOA and employer buyers need pre/post.
  9. Decide what stays free. Community cohort can stay free. Use the same modules for paid employer / WIOA / membership. Stop implying there is only one offer.

Short version: You have a good 6-week community program built as digital literacy. The suggestion is the same 6 weeks, rebuilt as AI-assisted independence. Change the spine from Phone → Safety → Zoom → Doctor → Bank → Social to AI win → AI-era safety → People → Health → Money/Work (fork) → Prove it. Keep the room, the 12 hours, the peer facilitator, and the forms. Rewrite the week titles, put one AI action in every session, add the teaching script and the Week 5 fork. That is the whole gap.
