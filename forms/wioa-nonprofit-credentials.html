What it takes to operate 50+TechBridge as a WIOA-qualified nonprofit

WIOA-eligible on a brochure is not the same as being on a state’s Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL). 501(c)(3) status is not the same as a career-school license. Texas MBE is a for-profit credential. The working model is two entities: keep Learn More Technologies LLC (MBE + invoices) and add a Texas nonprofit sidecar (grants + free seats). Do not convert the LLC.

2. Recommended legal structure (do this, not a conversion)

Entity A — keep. Learn More Technologies LLC dba 50+TechBridge. For-profit. Holds Texas MBE. Invoices employers, libraries, boards, and keynotes. Signs site-license and facilitator contracts. Applies to Texas ETPL as the training provider unless counsel puts the school license on the nonprofit instead — pick one provider of record and stick to it.

Entity B — add. New Texas nonprofit corporation (suggested working name distinct from the LLC DBA, e.g. 50+TechBridge Foundation / Digital Pioneer Institute — lawyer confirms name). Files IRS Form 1023. Receives grants. Buys free seats from the LLC or scholarships learners. Never the MBE vehicle.

Name collision. The LLC already uses 50+TechBridge as a DBA. Do not put the identical assumed name on the charity without counsel. Boards, banks, and SAM.gov treat two entities with the same public name as a red flag. One brand, two legal names, a written license/services agreement between them.

Interim if the IRS letter is slow: fiscal sponsorship under an existing 501(c)(3) can receive grants now. The sponsor, not LMT, is the legal grantee. Use only with a written fiscal-sponsor agreement.

Who is “WIOA-qualified”? The learner is determined eligible by the career center. The provider is qualified when (a) on the state ETPL for ITAs, or (b) under a board contract for cohort / OJT / incumbent-worker / customized training. You cannot self-certify either one.

B. Career school license — the Texas ETPL gate

Texas Education Code Chapter 132: almost all career training requires a TWC Certificate of Approval unless TWC grants a written exemption. A 501(c)(3) letter is evidence for an exemption request; it is not the exemption. Local boards will not send you to statewide ETPL without the license or the TWC exemption letter. C. WIOA / ETPL in Texas
  1. Call Workforce Solutions Capital Area (Austin local board) as the first board of record. Other Texas boards can use a statewide listing later if the program is on the Targeted Occupation List.
  2. Board issues EDvera access. You upload provider, campus, and program files.
  3. Attach TWC Certificate of Approval or TWC exemption letter. Registered apprenticeship is the other statutory path — not yours.
  4. Map each paid program to a SOC / target occupation. Attach employer support letter or an employer advisory committee (boards commonly require one or the other).
  5. Submit the Student Data Report (all students in the program, not only WIOA). New programs: file what you have from community cohorts and say the course is new where data are thin.
  6. Local board recommends → TWC adds the program to the statewide ETPL. Listing ≠ referrals. ITA caps are set locally (often roughly $7k–$9k per participant in many Texas regions — confirm with Capital Area before you print a price).
D. Texas extras people skip
  • Workers’ compensation — optional for many private Texas employers, but venues, boards, and grants will require a certificate. Buy it if you have any employee (facilitator).
  • General liability + additional insured — libraries and senior centers will require COI naming them. Professional / educators’ liability for instruction. Cyber / PII if you store WIOA data.
  • Unemployment tax — TWC UI account if you have employees.
  • CMBL / Texas HUB — state-agency bidding. HUB is for-profit; keep it on the LLC if eligible. City of Austin MBE (if that is the current cert) stays on the LLC.
  • Site MOUs — written facility use with APL branches, AARP-affiliated centers, Foundation Communities. ETPL “campus” addresses must match where you actually teach.
5. Massachusetts — only if you actually operate or fundraise there

Do not treat Massachusetts as a brochure line. The moment you solicit donations, hire, lease, or teach in MA, clocks start. A foreign (Texas) charity must register with the MA Secretary of the Commonwealth within 10 days of commencing business and with the Attorney General before charitable work or fundraising. B. Private occupational school license (G.L. c. 112 §263)

Massachusetts licenses private post-secondary occupational schools — for-profit and nonprofit. Step 1 is always the License Determination with the Division of Occupational Licensure (Office of Private Occupational School Education). Do not skip to ETPL and assume you are fine.

  • If DOL says you must be licensed: Office of the State Auditor financial / surety review → ePLACE application → nonprofit application fee $1,250 (for-profit is $2,500) → surety bond in the OSA amount → enrollment agreement (G.L. c. 255 §13K) → catalog / policies → instructor CORI + SORI + certification packets → building and fire inspection if you have a MA facility → DOL site visit. Incomplete applications sit open 9 months, then die.
  • Distance / online exemption: schools that provide only distance education and have no place of business in the Commonwealth are typically outside the license. Flying in to teach at a Boston library, senior center, or MassHire site is in-person MA delivery — get the License Determination in writing before you advertise a Massachusetts cohort.
  • Corporations that were not formed as schools may need to amend articles with SOS to include educational/school purposes.
C. MassHire ETPL (TrainingPro)
  1. Register in TrainingPro (MDCS). Provider review is state; course review is the local MassHire Workforce Board.
  2. Applications / renewals are due before June 1 each year. Miss it and you wait a cycle.
  3. New providers (and Trade / Section 30) are told to include course performance data from the past two years. If the course is genuinely new, say so in TrainingPro and start tracking the day the first MA or online cohort runs. Minimums commonly cited: completion ~70% plus entered-employment vs. local unemployment — confirm the current MDCS ETPL memo before you apply.
  4. Same occupational-alignment problem as Texas: the MassHire board must see a job, a credential, and a price, not a community AI workshop.

Practical sequence for MA: Do not open a Massachusetts site until Texas ETPL is live and one facilitator exists. When you do: SOS foreign qualify → AGO charity + solicitation certificate → DOL License Determination → then TrainingPro. Fundraising emails to MA donors without the AGO certificate is the fastest way to get a problem.

7. Suggested order (do not file Massachusetts first)
  1. This week: Confirm SAM.gov / UEI on the LLC. Confirm the exact name on the Texas MBE certificate. Start a cohort outcomes spreadsheet (completion, credential, employment) from existing sign-in/surveys.
  2. Counsel call: Dual-entity plan, name of the charity, related-party agreement, whether the LLC or the nonprofit will be the ETPL provider of record.
  3. Form Entity B: Texas SOS Form 202 → EIN → bylaws/policies → board of 3 → IRS Form 1023. Fiscal sponsor only if a grant cannot wait.
  4. TWC Career Schools: Exemption request (or Certificate of Approval if they say no). Do this before promising boards a listing date.
  5. Workforce Solutions Capital Area: EDvera packet, employer letter, Student Data Report, ITA-range prices. Then statewide ETPL.
  6. Comptroller AP-204 after the IRS determination letter. Open a charity bank account. Written seat-purchase agreement: charity buys free seats from the LLC.
  7. Massachusetts (later): SOS foreign qualify → AGO Charity Portal + Certificate of Solicitation → DOL License Determination → TrainingPro by the June 1 cycle, with two years of data or a documented new-course explanation.

Bottom line: You already have the operating company, the MBE, the footprint, and the teaching system. You do not yet have a charity, a TWC school determination, or an ETPL listing. Those three — plus SAM.gov confirmed — are the licenses that turn “WIOA-eligible” copy into money. Keep the LLC. Add the nonprofit. Do not convert.
