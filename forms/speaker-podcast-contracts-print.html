Business Model

Government contracts fund the proof. AGENTIC50 builds the reputation. Brian gets the stages.

Goal: establish Brian McKinney as a global speaker on AgeTech and 50+ AI. The podcast is the booking engine. Public contracts pay for delivery, data, and staff so Brian is not trapped in every classroom.

Brian is the speaker. Learn More Technologies is the machine that makes him bookable. Training is not the destination. Training is the evidence a conference, ministry, or brand needs before they put him on a global stage.

If Brian is in the classroom five days a week, the speaker brand dies. Contracts must buy other people’s delivery, not more of his hours.

2. Who pays for what

Government / workforce money pays for curriculum delivery (WIOA, boards, libraries, aging network); completions and outcomes reports; facilitator pay and bilingual materials; “dissemination” (a keynote or podcast season written into the scope); MBE / HUB set-aside training procurements.

That money does not pay Brian to become famous. It pays for Pioneer counts, completion rates, and org deployments to stay true and grow. Those numbers are what a London or DC booker checks.

Speaking fees and sponsor money pay for the brand: travel, video, media kit, AGENTIC50 production, a booking page that looks international.

Do not mix the two. Contracts = fuel and proof. Fees = status.

3. AGENTIC50 as a booking show

Keep the 50+ learner audience. Add a second listener: the person who books rooms. Repeat this episode pattern:

  1. Pioneer — a graduate who got the job, the portal, the confidence (proof)
  2. Buyer — workforce director, library system, HR, state aging, conference chair (they can hire you)
  3. Peer — AgeTech founder, aging leader, another speaker (status transfer)

Every episode ends with: “If you run a conference, workforce board, or AgeTech program — this is how you bring this into the room.” Then the speaking calendar link.

Production rules that serve the stage
  • One season = one keynote. Season title matches the talk ($76 Trillion Mistake, Ageism + AI, AI in One Hour).
  • Cut 5 clips per episode (30–45 seconds, captions). LinkedIn first — that is where bookers live.
  • After a contract cohort, record 2 Pioneer episodes from that site. The contract paid for the story.
  • Guest ask: “Who chairs the conference you just named?” Book that person next.
  • Put a guest quote on the speaker one-sheet, not only Brian’s.

The show wins when a stranger emails to book a keynote they heard on episode 12 — not when download counts look busy.

4. Which government money funds this

Stay in NAICS 611420, 611430, 624310. MBE + WIOA-eligible is the wedge. Chase contracts that include training + reporting + a public briefing.

First wave (Texas)
  • Workforce boards / Workforce Solutions — WIOA Title I Adult and Dislocated Worker; ITAs; ETPL or board contract
  • Library systems — digital literacy / city recitals (Austin Public Library already exists)
  • City/county aging and workforce RFPs; HUB/MBE set-asides
  • Community college adult ed (WIOA Title II) as a subcontractor first
Second wave (funds the speaker on purpose)
  • Write into every SOW: one leadership briefing or keynote + outcomes one-pager + optional AGENTIC50 episode with consented participants
  • State digital-equity / broadband-adjacent skills (partner; do not prime a huge grant alone)
  • ACL / area agencies on aging — AI + independence / scam prevention (Week 2)
  • DOL-aligned AI literacy (the TEGL lane already on the site)

Do not chase first: giant federal primes with no past-performance packet; GSA Schedule before UEI/SAM and two clean local contracts; grants that require Brian to teach every session; anything that funds “podcasting” as the deliverable. Fund training; treat the show as dissemination.

Housekeeping: SAM.gov + UEI, one-page capability statement, past performance (APL + current orgs), price list that matches ITA ranges, ETPL if not done, facilitator bench so a yes does not consume Brian.

6. Offer stack
  1. Free: AGENTIC50 + three free LMS lessons (Welcome, Talk to AI, Don’t get scammed)
  2. Contract: Digital Pioneer Core by facilitators; outcomes report; one staff briefing
  3. Stage: Three named talks only. Floor the fee. Virtual Lunch & Learn is the door; the keynote is the product
  4. After the stage: Paid cohort or brand briefing — never “I’ll train for exposure”

Bookers should see: podcast + Pioneer numbers + MBE/WIOA + a filmable classroom.

7. 90-day sequence
  1. Lock the speaker sentence: “Brian McKinney is the AgeTech speaker on 50+ AI — host of AGENTIC50, founder of the program workforce boards can actually buy.”
  2. Rebuild the podcast calendar around buyer guests (3 boards, 2 library systems, 1 conference chair, 2 Pioneers).
  3. One capability statement + SAM/UEI if not done.
  4. Two contract conversations a week using the 90-minute pilot + outcomes page — scope includes a briefing.
  5. Hire or certify one facilitator so the next yes does not consume Brian.
  6. Clip every episode to LinkedIn with the booking link.
  7. Raise the public speaking floor so the site matches a global speaker, not a $500 hobby.
8. Do not
  • Make AGENTIC50 a course. The LMS is the course. The show is reputation.
  • Take a training contract that requires Brian on site every week.
  • Guest only Pioneers. They cannot book a state SHRM or an international AgeTech stage.
  • Spend contract dollars on a new website before ETPL/SAM and a facilitator.
  • Call yourself a global speaker while the public price still says $500.

Run the company by this line: Government pays LMT to prove 50+ AI works. The podcast tells that story to people who own stages. Brian gets on those stages. Everything else is support.
